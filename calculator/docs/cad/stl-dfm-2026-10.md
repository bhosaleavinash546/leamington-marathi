# Geometric DFM on STL uploads (Oct 2026)

Until this change, an STL upload got a cost but no geometric DFM: the kernel cannot read a mesh, so `cad.ts` skipped the
job. Now the job reads the STL itself, recovering the faces a mesh hides and running the kernel's own feature
definitions on them. The commodity rule pack, pricing and report are unchanged: an STL part goes through the same
`analyseGeometricDFM` as a STEP.

Costing is not touched. The STL costing path (`stl-parser.ts`, the questions, the rules) is the same as before; this
change only adds the findings.

## How it works

`server/utils/mesh-features.ts` (pure TypeScript, rays through three-mesh-bvh):

1. **Weld and orient** the triangle soup. Orientation comes from the signed volume.
2. **Flat regions.** Triangles are grown across edges while they stay coplanar.
3. **Cylinders, by region growing.**
   - A seed region and a neighbour it turns into (0.5–36°) give an axis.
   - A Kåsa circle fit on the members' vertices gives the radius.
   - The cylinder grows while each new region's vertices sit on it and its normal points radially.
   - The fit is refitted as it grows. A partial cylinder needs ≥ 25° of sweep and a real length.
   - A seed tries its sharpest-turning neighbour first. The next neighbours are tried only for a full turn: where a boss
     meets a round tangentially, the round's first row turns more than the boss's next strip.
4. **Tori are not fillets.** A torus (an edge round, a root round) tessellates as short cylinders whose axes turn round
   a ring. A sector is dropped when either:
   - it touches a sector of the same radius whose axis is 2–45° from its own; or
   - three or more same-radius partial cylinders have axes in one plane, tangent to one circle at their mid-points (two
     sectors fix the circle and a third confirms it; two straight fillets meeting at a corner also fit a pair, so a pair
     alone never counts).

   The kernel types these faces as tori too.
5. **What is left** is a plane, or a facet of a curved surface. A facet is part of a strip pattern, or a lone triangle
   that joins smoothly. One smooth patch of facets becomes one free-form face, which gets no feature, as the kernel
   types it.
6. **The kernel's definitions, ported** (`_extract_manufacturing_features`, `_cylinder_features`, `_release_table`):
   - hole or boss at ≥ 0.83 of a turn; otherwise a fillet with concavity and sweep;
   - coaxial pieces re-joined on the axis line;
   - through or blind by the same breakout ring;
   - single-ray wall thickness;
   - the two-half release test, the cavity probe and undercut regions;
   - the draw axis with the fewest undercuts (ties go to the largest silhouette);
   - hot spots at more than 2 × the median, with `wallAnalysisValid` at fill < 0.55.

The DFM job (`dfm-job-runner.ts::stlPart`) runs this in a worker thread (`mesh-features-worker.ts`). The kernel runs out
of process for the same reason: 216k triangles take 14 s. The job applies the STEP path's thickness clean-up (readings
outside 0.3–3 × 2·V/S are dropped) and prices in the same country book. Meshes above 300,000 triangles are skipped, with
the reason stated (`MAX_TRIANGLES`; 0.9 GB at 216k, on a 2 GB VM). A viewer-quality export of a real part is
2–30k triangles.

**The viewer.** The job stores the face of every triangle, in file order, with its report. They are served on their own
route (`GET /api/dfm/jobs/:id/mesh-faces`) and never inside the report, which travels with costings and exports. The
screen hands them to the 3D viewer (`setMeshFaces`, `src/ui/mesh-faces.ts`), on CAD to COST's viewers only. A finding's
"show me", face picking, face types and wall thickness then work on the mesh. Before, those tools said "STL is mesh-only".

## Validation against the kernel

`npx tsx scripts/stl-dfm-validate.ts` measures each STEP with the kernel and exports it to STL at the viewer's
tessellation (17°) and a coarse CAD export (29°) (`scripts/stl-export.py`). It then compares holes, bosses and concave
fillets (matched on axis line and diameter within 1 %), through / blind, depth, draw, planes, undercuts, and the
findings each feature set produces through the same rule pack. Run 28 Oct 2026, viewer tessellation:

| Part | Holes | Bosses | Concave fillets (matched, extra) | Undercuts | Findings STL v kernel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hydraulic manifold | 13/13 | — | 4/4, 6 | 0 v 0 | 11 v 9 (2 non-preferred Ø) |
| Part1 | 15/15 | — | 12/22, 1 | 4 v 4 | **6 v 6** |
| Stepped shaft | 1/1 | 5/5 | — | 0 v 0 | **0 v 0** |
| Casting bracket | 10/10 | 1/1 | 14/31, 17 | 0 v 0 | 23 v 25 |
| PRCR002 (stub axle) | 23/24 | 3/8 | 5/28, 80 | 12 v 8 | 105 v 58 |
| Steering knuckle | 12/14 | 2/4 | 23/61, 46 | 2 v 1 | 28 v 43 |
| Hub flange | 7/7 | 2/2 | — | 0 v 0 | **0 v 0** |
| ECU cover | 4/4 | 4/4 | 0/8, 2 | 8 v 8 | **48 v 48** |
| Cable clip | 1/1 | — | — | 2 v 2 | 21 v 17 (4 rib thickness) |
| Seat bracket | 0 extra 10 | — | 10/24, 57 | 0 v 0 | **0 v 0** |
| Sprocket (modelled) | 1/1 | — | **18/18**, 0 | 0 v 0 | **0 v 0** |

What it says:

- **Prismatic and moulded parts match.** Holes, bosses, through / blind, draw and undercuts match, and so do the
  findings on 6 of the 11 parts.
- **Free-form castings and forgings do not.** On PRCR002, extra "fillets" are torus sectors the ring test does not
  catch, giving sharp-internal-corner 20 v 6. On the knuckle, 38 of the kernel's 61 fillets are on surfaces too curved
  to read from the facets.
- **The STL report of a cast or forged part is indicative.** The note on every STL report says so.
- **The sprocket's tooth form reads as the kernel reads it.**
  - Each roller seat is a concave cylinder of 284.6°, r 4.018 mm: less than a turn, so a "fillet", not a hole.
  - The STL gives the same sweep and radius on both tessellations.
  - The tooth tips (short convex arcs of the outside diameter) read as planes on the mesh. No rule reads them.

The sprocket is `scripts/sprocket-fixture.py` (OCP): ANSI #40 chain, 18 teeth, approximate B29.1 proportions, stated in
the file.

On the coarse 29° export, holes drop where a facet passes 36°: manifold 11/13, Part1 11/15. Fillets drop more, which is
a stated limit. A hole tessellated coarser than 36° a facet is not recognised, and a polygonal prism of ten or more sides
reads as a cylinder.

## Tests and live check

- `tests/stl-dfm.test.ts` covers:
  - synthetic solids (tube, straight fillet, revolved root round, a tube too coarse, binary and ASCII STL);
  - three real parts and the sprocket against the kernel's own features, recorded in `tests/fixtures/stl-dfm/*.kernel.json`
    by `scripts/stl-dfm-validate.ts --fixtures tests/fixtures/stl-dfm` (the sprocket via
    `CV_STL_EXTRA=<abs>/Sprocket_ANSI40_18T.step:machining:steel`);
  - the worker;
  - the DFM job on an STL (findings, and faces kept out of the report).
- `npx tsx e2e/stl-dfm-live.ts` runs a real server and browser. The ECU cover STL is uploaded on CAD to COST and answered
  injection moulding / plastic / PA66-GF30. The run then checks that the job is queued for the STL, that its findings
  equal the kernel's on the STEP, that the faces arrive on their own route, and that the viewer's face tools switch on.

## Not done

- **Routing an STL.** Without AI, CAD to COST asks the route (sheet metal / injection moulding / machining) for the ECU
  cover STL, while its STEP is routed to moulding. The mesh features could feed the routing evidence, but that is a
  costing change and was not made here.
- **Tori.** PRCR002's bore-edge rounds still leave sectors the ring test misses: sectors whose mid-points do not fit one
  circle because the fit took a piece of the neighbouring face.
