# Manufacturing / process engineer review: CostVision should-cost reports

Reviewer role: manufacturing and process engineer, covering foundry, forge, machine shop, press shop, moulding and extrusion plants.
Scope: all 21 pages of the UK stub-axle PDF read line by line, and the other 14 UK PDFs plus their workbooks
(`3-Operations`, `7-Checks`, `8-Routes`) read for process content. I checked the China stub axle only for process text and
currency. I did not run the tool. Where I re-did the arithmetic I used the report's own numbers. Every "realistic" figure below is
my shop-floor judgement and is marked as such. A supplier's actual cycle or quote would replace it.

**Verdict in one paragraph.** The tool's chain is traceable and mostly picks the right process family. The forging line takt,
the press-sizing logic, the IM cavitation ranking, the sheet unfold and the Al-extrusion planning are good engineering. Three
things a supplier would dispute on sight, however:
1. **Machining cycle times are 3–10× too long on every part that machines.** Finishing passes run at 40 cm²/min in aluminium
   (25 cm²/min in iron). Each hole also carries 0.15 min plus a 0.25 min "bore pass" of fixed time. These minutes inflate
   machine time, labour and tool wear together. This is the largest £ error in the set: about £8–12 on the £41.24 stub axle.
2. **Equipment is not sized to 200,000 parts a year.** The tool used a 30 moulds/h semi-automatic sand line with wood-pattern
   life, a 6-minute manual fettle, an autoclave with 500-shot composite tools, and 92 roto moulds. Nobody runs those at 200,000
   a year. The report never states how many machines or lines the volume needs. That count is the one check that would expose
   the problem.
3. **"Tooling" mixes the first investment with programme-life consumption.** For example, "Tooling £731,491" on the stub axle
   is really about £26k of first tooling plus £706k of replacement pattern sets at a wood-pattern life. The route-comparison
   payback maths treats wear parts as capital.

## C. Top 10 changes, in order

1. **Fix the machining time base** (`FINISH_RATE_CM2_PER_MIN.wall` 40 cm²/min applied to every planar face, plus the per-hole
   fixed allowances). Use a face-mill rate for planar faces and chip-to-chip tool changes, and validate against two supplier
   cycle sheets. This is worth about £8–12 on the stub axle, £5–8 on the manifold and £2–5 on the knuckle, hub and bracket.
   Today every machined part is overstated, and every lever and DFM £ inherits the error.
2. **Print a capacity block on page one for every part**: machines or lines needed at the programme volume, loading %, shifts.
   It would have exposed the stub axle's 17 HMCs and 123%-loaded moulding line, the 92 roto moulds and the 9–10 autoclaves.
   It is the cheapest sanity check a process engineer has.
3. **Choose equipment class and tooling life by volume.** Use automatic flaskless lines above ~50k moulds/yr, pattern life by
   pattern material (never price aluminium at wood life), automated fettling at volume, metal composite tools when the cycles
   exceed a composite tool's life, cryogenic deflash, and forged gear blanks. The stub axle tooling headline drops from £731k
   to ~£30–60k, and the composites tooling from £25.3M to ≤ £3M.
4. **Split tooling into "initial investment" and "replacement / perishable £/part"** on page one, in §2 and in §9b. Compute
   payback only on the initial line. Hob and wheel wear and die-set consumption are not capital.
5. **Add a page-one route alert** whenever a feasible route is ≥ 20% cheaper at the programme volume (roto → blow −84%,
   thermoform → IM −49%), and add the material-change alternative a buyer actually faces (stub axle: forged steel; knuckle:
   ductile casting; composites: PCM / HP-RTM / SMC).
6. **Run geometric DFM only on the faces the process forms.** Exclude the costing's machined faces from draft, fillet, undercut
   and section checks on castings and forgings, and judge setups and compound angles against the costed machine. Today most
   "MAJOR" findings on the stub axle and knuckle are artefacts of the finished model.
7. **Remove fake levers**: "Attack the Bottleneck −20%", "Consumables ×0.8", the £0.00 lever, Multi-cavity ×2 applied to
   machining and fettling (£5.93 claimed on the bracket), and lights-out without automation capex. Keep only levers tied to a
   named physical change that the stack re-costs.
8. **Fix the route-comparison defects**: itemise every consumable (knuckle precision forging's unexplained £34.52/part), never
   down-grade an answered safety material (stub axle → grey-iron plate), drop non-credible rows from the core, and correct the
   "grey iron" text on a ductile part.
9. **Ask the missing process questions and stop ignoring evidence**: safety and grade on sheet metal (seat locking bracket in
   DC04), a named grade in the file (hub flange "C45" costed as 38MnVS6 with no heat treatment), leak test / port / cleanliness
   on manifolds, technical-part blow moulding (no "container capacity" for a duct). Make checkbox fields print what the costing
   actually applied (vent-pipe leak test "on" with no test op; thermoformer "rotary on" with a single-station cycle).
10. **Complete the routing for each commodity on page one, with a "Not included" list**: industrial wash after machining,
    cross-hole deburr, nodularity and hardness checks for safety ductile iron, gear bore/face hard turning, billet shear and
    induction-heater capital, blow-moulded end-cut, bagging consumables. Each one is either costed with its basis or listed
    as "Not included until answered", never silently absent.

### Where I am unsure
- The **realistic cycle times** I quote (rows 2–4) are shop-floor judgement. The direction and order of magnitude are sure
  (40 cm²/min face milling is ~10× too slow); the exact £ needs a supplier cycle sheet.
- **Al-extrusion die life** (40 t a die) may be right for a 4-void porthole die. I flag it for confirmation only.
- **HMC capex £185k / 13-yr life** is low but defensible. I would not change it ahead of the cycle-time fix.
- **Vent-pipe and thermoformer "on (edited)" flags**: I cannot tell from the report whether the display or the costing is
  wrong, only that they disagree.
- **Knuckle precision forging £34.52 consumables**: the report does not show what it is. It could be a mis-mapped die or NDT
  charge. Either way it must be itemised.
