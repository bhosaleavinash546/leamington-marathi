# Should-cost report review: Cost Engineering Director

**Role:** I sign off should-costs that buyers take into supplier negotiations and that the President sees.
**Read:** stub axle UK PDF (21 pp, all pages, line by line) + its workbook ops / traceability sheets; the other 14 UK PDFs
(executive page, commercial / tooling, rule basis, operations, §9b, §11–§14, findings); China stub axle (22 pp) for
currency and text. No files edited; app not run.

**Verdict.** The arithmetic is honest. Every bucket re-adds, the screen equals the report, and CN ¥142.19 = the UK report's
China row £16.01 at 8.88. **I would not sign the current PDF**, and the reason is not its length. On several parts the
page-one message is wrong or missing:
- On the casting bracket the top lever (£5.93, 34%) is not real.
- On the knuckle and the bracket, machining that may be worth more than the headline (knuckle: £13.05 machine time, 57.6
  min) is "NOT costed", and the reader is told only on page 9.
- The roto tank has a feasible route 84% cheaper. The executive page says "none could be re-costed".
- On the composites part, 37% of the price rests on one unsourced tool life of 500 parts, under a ±6.7% band.
- The "Material" line is 4.4× the metal on the stub axle (£11.98 shown, £2.71 of it iron), which is dangerous in an
  index-clause negotiation.
- The report prints the tool's own discarded higher tooling estimates (£32,500 / £98,000 / £126,000 "not used"). A supplier
  will quote those back at us.

Cut to the core and fix those, and the tool gives a buyer something defensible.

All £ effects below come from the report's own numbers unless I say "my judgement".
