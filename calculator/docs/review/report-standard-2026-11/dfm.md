# DFM / DFA expert review — CostVision should-cost reports (UK, 200k/yr, 5 yr, EXW)

Reviewer role: design for manufacture and assembly across casting, forging, machining, moulding, sheet, extrusion, composites, rubber.
Read: stub axle UK PDF in full (21 pp); the DFM/DFA section, levers (§12, exec page) and insights (§11) of the other 14 UK PDFs;
CN stub axle DFM/levers; workbook sheet lists; screenshots of the stub axle and knuckle. To confirm what the text implied I read the
rule code (`src/engine/dfm-geometry/commodities/*.ts`, `cost-impact.ts`, `feature-machining.ts`, `machining-time.ts`). I edited no files.

**Verdict in one paragraph.** The DFM engine measures geometry well and is honest about what it does not price. As a design
engineer's tool, though, it often checks the wrong surfaces for the process. The worst case: the casting draft rule flags the zero-draft
flats that the costing itself defines as the machined faces. Several findings use die-casting, blow-moulding or prototype-shop wording on
parts made another way. Severities are inflated: on the stub axle 7 of 9 findings are MAJOR, and none of them blocks the process or changes the price much.
The £ figures attached to DFM findings and levers are mostly whole feature costs, impossible merges or rule-of-thumb multipliers
(−20 %, ×0.6, ×0.8, ×2 cavities), not achievable design deltas. The biggest one is the casting bracket "multi-cavity" lever, which
claims £5.93 (34 %). It halves the CNC machining and fettling as well as the moulding, on a pattern that is already 4-up. For a design
engineer the actionable content is small: the ECU cover draft and core pins, the manifold cross-holes, the stub axle's section change
on as-cast faces, and the seat bracket formability and nesting. Six commodities have no DFM section at all, the sheet-metal pack has two
trivial rules, and the Excel workbook carries no DFM findings at all.

## C. Top 10 changes, in order

1. **Check process faces only.** Run draft, fillet and section rules on as-cast / as-forged / as-moulded faces, and exclude every face the
   costing machines (faced rows, holes, cored bores, turned area). Today the casting draft rule flags exactly the zero-draft flats the costing
   machines (`nearNetMachiningTime`). Print a machined / as-cast face map on the core page.
2. **Fix the lever engine before anything else reaches page 2.** Scope multi-cavity to the forming op and only below the rule's own cavitation
   (casting bracket £5.93 → ≤ £0.5). Withdraw "−20 % bottleneck", "×0.6 lights-out" and "×0.8 consumables" unless each is tied to a named physical
   change with its capex. Never list a £0 lever.
3. **A DFM £ must be the design-addressable delta.** Do not use "the features' whole cost" or "every size merged onto one tool". Align
   thresholds with the costing's own model (peck past 5×D) and real tooling (DIN 338 jobber reach). The stub axle deep-hole £1.29 is £0 in its own model.
4. **Re-grade severity** as BLOCKING / COST / RISK / INFO. MAJOR today is applied to unpriced, non-blocking findings (7 of 9 on the stub axle).
5. **Process-correct text and sources.** No die wear or ejection on sand, no NADCA on sand, no pinch-off or fuel-tank text on roto or ducts, no
   feeds-and-speeds advice on roto, no runners in thermoforming. Replace search-engine and prototype-shop citations with production standards
   (EN 12890, ISO 8062-3, DIN 7523-2 / EN 10243-1, DIN 338, ISO 866). Remove code identifiers and developer notes (knuckle).
6. **Wire findings to the cost paths that exist.** Moulding: the governing wall drives cooling (ECU 4.5 mm vs 2.5 mm, up to ~+£0.20/part).
   Casting and forging: draft adds metal. Casting undercut → core cost (reconcile 8 vs 32 undercut faces). Manifold cross-holes → a deburr operation.
7. **Add the missing packs.** Sheet metal: hole-to-edge, hole-to-bend, relief, flange, radius, all from the developed blank that already exists;
   fix the export race that dropped the seat bracket's DFM. For the six commodities with no pack, print the checks a design engineer must make (§B).
8. **Add a process-at-volume check** to DFM and route comparison: hand-layup composites at 200k/yr (£25.34/part tooling), bar-slice gear blanks
   at 200k/yr, roto with 92 moulds. State "this process is unsuited to the volume" and cost the volume route.
9. **Fix feature recognition that drives findings and £.** Treat stepped-bore lands as one bore, not 10 bores or 10 sizes. Recognise centre
   holes (Ø3.15 is ISO 866, not "non-stock"). Treat shallow lands of any Ø as spot-faces. Do not ream through clearance holes. A coaxial axis at
   one angle is not "compound".
10. **Core report layout for DFM.** One page: at most 6 findings, each with what / where (one picture) / why it matters on THIS process /
    re-costed £ or "risk, not priced" / owner (design, foundry, toolmaker). Move face lists, sources, "not checked" lists and 4C to the
    audit pack. Remove Boothroyd DFA (no assembly, no £, wrong for heavy parts). Add a `9-DFM` sheet to the workbook.
