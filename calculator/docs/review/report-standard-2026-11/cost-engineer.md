# Should-cost report review — SENIOR COST ENGINEER

Lens: the cost model itself — is every driver present and right for the commodity, double counts / gaps, plausibility of
assumptions against practice, which unsourced constants carry the £, and what a decision-maker needs on page one versus
the audit pack. All arithmetic below is re-done from the reports' own figures (UK, 200k/yr, 5 yr, EXW). "×1.21" = the
report's own overhead 12 % then margin 8 % (1.12 × 1.08 = 1.2096) applied to a change in a factory-cost line.

**Overall verdict on the stub axle (PRCR002, £41.24).** The arithmetic closes (I re-added every bucket: 11.98 + 6.99 + 14.07 +
0.73 = 33.77; × 12 % = 4.05; + 0.36 = 38.19; × 8 % = 3.05; total 41.24). The casting half is about right in TOTAL: metal
£2.71 + foundry conversion £12.29 (moulding £2.48, melt labour £0.60, fettling £2.76, energy £1.69, sand £0.15, cores £3.09,
blast £0.75, NDT £0.77) = £15.00 for a 9.08 kg casting = £1.65/kg before OH/margin, ~£2.00/kg after — inside the £2–3/kg a UK
automotive ductile-iron safety casting fettled, blasted and MPI'd sells for. The individual foundry lines are mis-shaped for
200k/yr (a semi-automatic 30 moulds/h line with a crew of 4, 6 min manual fettling, 65 pattern sets) but they partly offset.
The machining half is where the figure is heavy: 28.1 min of cutting (+1.2 min lathe handling, 2.5 min deburr) for a stub axle
is roughly twice what a production twin-pallet HMC + lathe cell runs; with tool wear and OH/margin it carries ~£19.5 of the
£41.24. My judgement: the part should cost ~£30–35 in the UK, not £41 — the gap is mostly cutting rates. I cannot prove this
without a CAM time or a supplier cycle; I say so in each row.

## B. Commodity matrix (cost-engineer view)

"Page 1" = what the 6–8 page core report must show for a cost engineer / buyer to accept or challenge the number.
"Audit" = the rest. "Checks" = DFM / quality / process checks that apply, and those that must not.

## C. Top 10 changes (in order)

1. **Fix the cutting-time model before any machined figure is shown to a buyer.** Scale only the cutting term by
   machinability (not the fixed approach / retract / tool-change allowances), and replace drill / bore / face-mill rates with
   sourced vc–f by family. Expected effect: stub axle −£8–11, casting bracket ~−£4, every machined route lower. Until done,
   page 1 must say "machining minutes engineering-typical, conservative — confirm with a supplier cycle".
2. **Composites tool life by tool material** (500 → steel / Invar thousands; bound replacements by cycles each tool sees).
   −£27/part (39 %) on the hat stiffener; tooling should not be the #1 line.
3. **Cavity / multi-up levers only on the forming op, with the machine re-sized and existing impressions read.** Removes
   the false £5.93 (34 %) on the casting bracket and £0.18 (19.5 %) on the air duct; drop £0.00 levers and rule-of-thumb inputs
   (the ×0.8 consumables lever) — they are not "re-costed" in any useful sense.
4. **Re-cut page 1's cost lines:** Metal (index-linked) · Bought consumables & services · Perishable tooling · Energy ·
   Conversion by stage (casting / forging / moulding, then machining) · Tooling investment (separate, with and without in the
   piece price) · Overhead · Margin. Today "Material £11.98" on the stub axle is £2.71 of iron; the gear's £607k "tooling" is
   wheel and hob wear.
5. **Overhead and margin: label and split.** "12 % — CostVision default, not sourced" on page 1; split factory overhead (on
   conversion) from SG&A and profit; do not put OH / margin on metal pass-through for extrusion (−£4.94 shown as OH+margin on
   the battery rail billet) — show conversion £/kg instead.
6. **Foundry process by volume:** automatic moulding line above ~50k/yr (crew, moulds/h, rate incl. sand plant), fettling
   minutes by weight / alloy / automation (6 min manual is jobbing), pattern life by pattern material (65 sets / £731k →
   2–4 sets / ~£100k), repeat tool sets without design / CAM / tryout, core cost from the measured cored volume.
7. **Add the missing process steps on machined routes:** machining scrap on cast / forged + machine (1–2 % of value,
   ~£0.3–0.6 on the stub axle), post-machining wash, gauges / check fixtures in tooling NRE, swarf credit, melt-shop and
   billet-heater / saw capital. List each in "Not included" until it is costed.
8. **Uncertainty: stop presenting ±8 % as if it were accuracy.** Page 1 says "Accuracy unmeasured"; rule-set cycle times and
   engineering-typical constants draw σ ≥ 0.15; the band and its method go to the audit pack.
9. **Make the rate book consistent with how the cell runs:** multi-shift depreciation life for a 6,000 h HMC (13 → ~8.7 yr,
   +£0.9/part), one shift pattern per routing (the lathe at 4,000 h, labour at a 2-shift premium beside a 3-shift HMC), a lathe
   priced as a lathe (not a VF-2), sourced wage pages; in other countries do not let at-par capital compete with sourced capital
   in the routing optimiser (China flips to 5 fixturings on VMCs for that reason).
10. **Core report = 6–8 pages built from what a cost engineer challenges:** page 1 headline (with / without tooling), cost
    lines per #4, weight cascade, the top 8 unsourced assumptions with their £, "Not included / to confirm" ranked by £;
    page 2 routing table (op · machine class · cycle · crew · £); page 3 tooling investment and life; page 4 sensitivity
    (rates AND assumptions) and regional summary (5 rows); page 5 route verdict in one paragraph; DFM limited to findings that
    move this costing, against the costed routing and only on as-cast faces where it is a casting rule. Move §4C features, §5
    build-ups, §6 traceability, §7 method, §9b dead routes, §10 carbon, §11 insights, DFA and "What was NOT checked" to the
    audit pack; remove unused scrap prices, £0.00 levers and generic reference-band insights.
