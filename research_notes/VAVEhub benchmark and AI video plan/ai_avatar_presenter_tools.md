# AI Presenter / Avatar Video Platforms for a Free, Single-Author Value Engineering Course (research as of 14 Oct 2026)

**Method note (read first):** Every vendor site (heygen.com, synthesia.io, colossyan.com, elevenlabs.io) was blocked by the network proxy. WebFetch returned DNS failures and curl got 403 on CONNECT. So **no official pricing or terms page was read directly**. All figures below come from WebSearch result snippets and summaries, mostly third-party 2026 pricing reviews, plus a few vendor help or blog pages that appeared in results. Third-party sources often disagreed, and those conflicts are recorded. Treat every price as "reported in 2026 by the cited source, retrieved 14 Oct 2026", and check it on the vendor page before quoting it in the final report. Prices are in USD unless stated.

## 1. Plans, prices and features (free tiers, limits, watermark, resolution, British voices, layouts, interactivity, SCORM, translation, API)

### Takeaway
For a solo author, the realistic entry tier is about **$24–30/month**: HeyGen Creator, Synthesia Starter or Colossyan Starter. Each removes the watermark and gives 1080p. The free tiers are watermarked or limited, and HeyGen's free tier is non-commercial. Interactivity (quizzes and branching) and SCORM are mostly mid or upper tier. Synthesia keeps SCORM for Enterprise. HeyGen documents SCORM export with quizzes and branching built in its AI Studio. Hour One was acquired by Wix in May 2025 and I could not confirm it still exists as a standalone product.

### Inferences
- A HeyGen Creator plan at about $24–29/month is the cheapest route to 1080p, unwatermarked, commercially licensed presenter video with a personal avatar and SCORM. A free course does not need SCORM unless it is distributed to LMSs.
- Synthesia's SCORM and interactivity sit at Creator or Enterprise, which is poor value for a solo free course. Its Starter plan at about 10 minutes/month is enough for roughly 2–4 short explainers a month.
- Colossyan's 5-minute cap on Starter is enough for 2–5 minute explainers. Its branching and SCORM strengths need Business.
- D-ID is cheapest but watermarked on Lite and mainly a talking-photo tool, so it is less suited to realistic training presenters.
- Interactivity such as quizzes and branching is arguably better kept in the course website, which already has quizzes, than built inside a vendor player. That avoids lock-in.

- British-accent voice counts per platform were not found in snippets. All three main platforms are generally known to offer en-GB voices, but no source here confirmed numbers.
- Gestures and slide/screen layouts per platform were not covered by the snippets, beyond Avatar V's shot variety.
- Hour One's current availability as a standalone product after the Wix acquisition is not confirmed.
- No API price list was found for any vendor.

## 2. Custom avatar ("digital twin"): requirements, consent, cost, quality, rights, revocation

### Takeaway
Every major vendor requires consent recorded by the person being cloned. HeyGen now needs only a single clip of about 15–30 seconds plus a consent video under 30 seconds. Synthesia offers a webcam "Express" personal avatar on Starter and Creator, and a studio avatar reportedly costs about £800–$1,000 extra. Synthesia documents that the avatar owner can request deletion at any time. HeyGen's deletion process for avatar data was not found, and users report deletion bugs.

### Cited Findings
- HeyGen: consent is required only for "digital_twin" avatars. Photo and prompt-generated avatars do not need it. — [HeyGen developer docs: avatar consent](https://developers.heygen.com/docs/avatar-consent)
- HeyGen: the consent video must be recorded by the person being cloned, even when someone else builds the avatar. It must be under 30 seconds, with clear lighting and sound, and show the same person as the training footage. — [HeyGen help centre](https://help.heygen.com/en/articles/12092609)
- HeyGen: "Upload a single 15-second clip and Avatar V builds a persistent personal avatar". HeyGen's blog recommends about 30 seconds of footage. — [HeyGen custom avatar](https://www.heygen.com/tool/custom-avatar); [HeyGen blog: digital twin](https://www.heygen.com/blog/how-to-create-digital-twin-avatar)
- HeyGen: the API can delete an avatar "look", but Studio avatar (model_index) types cannot be deleted via the API. Forum users report that deleted instant avatars reappear and the quota is not freed. — [HeyGen API ref](https://developers.heygen.com/reference/delete-avatar-look.md); [HeyGen developer forum](https://docs.heygen.com/discuss/675f1d6d649326001170734e)
- HeyGen moderation policy: a person depicted can contact the moderation team to request removal of content depicting them. — [HeyGen moderation policy](https://www.heygen.com/moderation-policy)
- Synthesia: "We will never create an AI avatar without someone's clear consent" (Alexandru Voica, Synthesia). Users consent to collection and use of their video and audio "to authenticate the personal avatar submission". Content is moderated by automated checks plus human review. — [Decrypt review](https://decrypt.co/243795/ai-video-avatars-synthesia-review)
- Synthesia Express/personal avatar: about 5 minutes of webcam recording, ready within hours according to a third-party account. Reported available on Starter and Creator. — [marcandrews.com avatar guide](https://marcandrews.com/synthesia-ai-avatars-guide-choose-customise-create/); [aiworthit](https://www.aiworthit.com/blog/synthesia-review/)
- Synthesia studio/custom avatar: reported at "upwards of £800 per avatar" or about $1,000/year on top of the plan. Sources conflict. — [aitoolsatlas](https://aitoolsatlas.ai/tools/synthesia/pricing); [aiworthit](https://www.aiworthit.com/blog/synthesia-review/)
- Synthesia avatar policy: "avatar owners have full control and can request their data be deleted from Synthesia at any time". Avatars stay in company workspaces after a user leaves unless deleted. — [Synthesia help: avatar policy](https://help.synthesia.io/en/articles/15453102-avatar-policy-foundation)
- Synthesia privacy (third-party summary): deletion requests go to privacy@synthesia.io. Biometric data is kept for the life of the account unless deletion is requested. UK, EU and California users have statutory deletion rights. — [ConductAtlas: Synthesia privacy](https://conductatlas.com/platform/synthesia/synthesia-privacy-policy/data-subject-rights-and-exercise-process/)
- Colossyan Starter reportedly includes 3 custom or "instant" avatars plus a voice clone. Counts conflict between sources. — [heyfish.ai](https://heyfish.ai/colossyan-review)
- ElevenLabs Professional Voice Cloning needs a live verification: you read a text captcha aloud, with 4 attempts, then manual review. Per one guide, you can only clone your own voice on your account. — [ElevenLabs docs: voices](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/voices); [techjacksolutions](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-tools/elevenlabs/elevenlabs-voice-cloning/)

### Inferences
- For a single author cloning himself, the consent step is trivial (a self-recorded consent clip). Budget about 1–2 hours for a well-lit, quiet recording session to get a good twin.
- As a UK-based author, he has UK GDPR rights to have his likeness and voice data deleted. Synthesia documents the route. With HeyGen, he should keep his own copy of the source footage and email support or privacy to delete, rather than relying on the UI.

### Gaps
- Official HeyGen and Colossyan personal-avatar data deletion and retention procedures were not found.
- No head-to-head realism or quality benchmark of twins across vendors was found, only vendor claims.
- Whether vendors may use the author's likeness or voice to train their models was not established from snippets.

## 3. Licensing, commercial and educational use, restrictions, moderation, labelling

### Takeaway
Paid plans generally give the user ownership or commercial use of the output, but free tiers often do not. HeyGen's free plan is non-commercial and HeyGen retains ownership; ElevenLabs Free has no commercial licence. All vendors ban impersonation and non-consensual likeness. Synthesia's stock avatars historically could not be used in paid social ads or on TV. From 2 August 2026, the EU AI Act Article 50 requires a visible label on realistic deepfake-style content published to EU audiences. A realistic avatar presenter very likely needs an "AI-generated presenter" disclosure, which is good practice anyway.

### Cited Findings
- HeyGen FAQ: paid subscribers own the videos they generate, subject to the ToS. For free-plan users HeyGen retains ownership, and videos may be used only for non-commercial purposes. — [HeyGen FAQ](https://www.heygen.com/faq)
- Synthesia: customers keep ownership of their uploads and of generated videos, conditional on compliance and payment, according to a third-party summary. Synthesia keeps the platform and "Synthesia Content". — [ConductAtlas: Synthesia IP](https://conductatlas.com/platform/synthesia/synthesia-terms-of-service/intellectual-property-ownership/)
- Synthesia MSA (v2021.10.01, may be outdated): bars content that Synthesia in its "full discretion" deems harmful, offensive, "unethical or controversial". Stock (non-exclusive) avatars may not be used for TV broadcast or promoted, boosted or paid social ads. — [Synthesia MSA 2021](https://www.synthesia.io/terms/master-service-agreement-v2021-10-01)
- Synthesia prohibits impersonation and obscene, defamatory or fraudulent content, according to an older snapshot hosted by Ofcom. Current terms list non-consensual depictions of real persons and deceptive authentic-appearing content as prohibited. — [Ofcom-hosted Synthesia doc](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0023/254840/Synthesia.pdf); [ConductAtlas: deepfake prohibition](https://conductatlas.com/platform/synthesia/synthesia-terms-of-service/prohibition-on-deepfakes-and-deceptive-synthetic-media/)
- Synthesia can suspend accounts for AUP violation or "reputational risk". Liability is capped at fees paid in the prior 12 months. — [ConductAtlas: Synthesia ToS](https://conductatlas.com/platform/synthesia/synthesia-terms-of-service/)
- ElevenLabs: the free plan has no commercial licence. Commercial use starts on Starter at $6/month. The Prohibited Use Policy bars impersonating political candidates or officials even with authorisation. — [bigvu](https://bigvu.tv/blog/elevenlabs-pricing-2026-plan-worth/); [techjacksolutions](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-tools/elevenlabs/elevenlabs-voice-cloning/)
- EU AI Act Article 50 took effect on 2 Aug 2026 and was not deferred by the Digital Omnibus. Deployers who publish a deepfake must add a visible or audible label at first exposure. The Commission's draft guidance defines deepfake broadly: AI-generated content resembling real or realistic people that could be mistaken for genuine. Fines go up to €15m or 3% of turnover. — [SRD Rechtsanwälte](https://www.srd-rechtsanwaelte.de/en/blog/deepfake-labelling-under-the-ai-act-what-the-new-eu-guidance-clarifies); [Lausen](https://lausen.com/en/section-504-of-the-ai-act-what-organisations-must-label-as-ai-content-from-august-2026/); [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/CSA_research_note_eu_ai_act_article_50_transparency_20260729-csa-styled-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- A free course is still "publishing". The safest posture is a paid plan for any presenter video, so there is no ownership or non-commercial ambiguity, plus a short on-screen "AI-generated presenter (digital twin of [author])" label, and avoiding third-party brand logos or real-company people in avatar scripts.
- The UK has no Article 50 equivalent, but a course reachable from the EU is plausibly in scope. Labelling also guards against the trust penalty found when learners discover a presenter is AI (see section 6).

### Gaps
- Current (2026) HeyGen, Synthesia and Colossyan ToS texts were not read directly. Any explicit clauses on educational use, brand logos and trademarks, and whether vendors may reuse customer videos were not confirmed.
- Whether the final Commission Article 50 guidance was published, and how it treats self-avatars, is unconfirmed.

## 4. Google NotebookLM Video Overviews and Audio Overviews

### Takeaway
NotebookLM turns uploaded sources into narrated, slide-style explainer videos with no human presenter. There are two formats: Explainer (longer) and Brief (short). Since Oct 2025 there are Nano Banana visual styles, and since mid-2026 a 60-second portrait "Short" format. Output is downloadable MP4. Google claims no ownership, but warns of inaccuracies, and there are no editing controls. It is useful for draft or supplementary recaps, not as the precise primary teaching video for a technical VE course.

### Cited Findings
- Oct 2025 upgrade: six Nano Banana visual styles (Watercolor, Papercraft, Anime, Whiteboard, Retro Print, Heritage) plus Classic and Auto-select. Two formats: **Explainer** ("structured, comprehensive") and **Brief** ("bite-sized"). Rolled out to Pro users first, then all users. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-labs/video-overviews-nano-banana/); [9to5Google Oct 2025](https://9to5google.com/2025/10/13/notebooklm-video-overviews-styles/)
- June–July 2026: "Short Video Overviews", 60-second portrait videos made with Nano Banana 2 Lite. **English only, Google AI Pro/Ultra subscribers aged 18+.** — [9to5Google Jun 2026](https://9to5google.com/2026/06/30/notebooklm-short-video-overviews/); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/7/create-60-second-educational-videos-with-notebooklm-s-nano-banana-2-lite/)
- A "Cinematic" Video Overview format is slower to generate (sometimes 30+ minutes), English only, 18+. — [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/what-is-notebooklm-cinematic-video-overviews); [kompozy review](https://kompozy.io/reviews/notebooklm)
- Languages: audio and video overviews were expanded to 80+ languages. The Short format is the exception. — [Wikipedia: NotebookLM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NotebookLM)
- Length: a typical Video Overview is about 6 minutes and about 20 slides for one source; one review says the cap is about 8 minutes. No official length setting was found. Audio Overview lengths are short (about 5+ minutes), default (about 10+) and long (about 20+), per NotebookLM's official X account. — [AI Goes to College](https://aigoestocollege.substack.com/p/notebooklm-gets-even-better-video); [kompozy](https://kompozy.io/reviews/notebooklm); [NotebookLM on X](https://x.com/NotebookLM/status/1924932327492248026)
- Quality caveats: Google warns that "AI-generated voices and visuals may contain inaccuracies or audio glitches". A reviewer found inaccurate information and skipped key data. Visuals can feel generic for technical content. Script, narrator, style and timeline cannot be edited. — [aethovpn guide](https://www.aethovpn.com/en/blog/ai-tools/use-notebooklm-video-overviews-check-coverage); [MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/notebooklm-video-overviews-learning/); [coursiv](https://coursiv.io/blog/notebooklm-video-overview)
- Export: MP4 download is possible and has been edited in Descript by educators. — [AI School Librarian](https://aischoollibrarian.substack.com/p/special-edition-from-text-to-teaching)
- Tier access conflicts: one source says it is on the free plan with an unpublished daily cap, another says it is a Plus feature. — [knowaidata](https://knowaidata.com/tools-guide/notebooklm-video-overviews-study-guide/); [kompozy](https://kompozy.io/reviews/notebooklm)
- Rights: a Google help page says Google won't claim ownership of original content users generate (the product is now also called "Gemini Notebook"). Users are responsible for rights in source materials and must follow Google's Generative AI Prohibited Use Policy and YouTube policies. — [Google NotebookLM help](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/16322204?hl=en); [Justia lawyer Q&A, not legal advice](https://answers.justia.com/question/2025/10/27/is-it-legal-to-upload-ai-generated-educa-1089667)
- Research use: a 2026 MDPI *Systems* paper evaluated adoption of AI-generated instructional videos made with NotebookLM. Only the title and DOI were seen. — [MDPI Systems 14(6):631](https://doi.org/10.3390/systems14060631)

### Inferences
- Since the author writes his own course material, NotebookLM is a near-zero-cost way to make module "recap" videos or podcasts from his own text. Because there are no editing controls and accuracy is not guaranteed, every output needs a technical review before publishing, and it is unsuitable where exact figures or FAST-diagram details matter.
- The voices are NotebookLM's own hosts, with no British-accent or author-voice control documented. That clashes with a British English, presenter-led brand.

### Gaps
- No source confirmed whether Video Overviews carry a visible watermark or SynthID marking.
- NotebookLM-specific terms on public or commercial publication were not read directly. The ownership statement comes from a Google help page as summarised in search.
- No official maximum length for Video Overviews was found.

## 5. ElevenLabs: voice cloning, British voices, pricing, commercial terms, combining with slides

### Takeaway
ElevenLabs Starter at about $6/month gives a commercial licence and Instant Voice Cloning. Creator at about $22/month adds Professional Voice Cloning, which is verified by a live captcha read. For a slides or screen-recording course, an ElevenLabs narration track over the author's own slides is the cheapest, most controllable and most accurate route. It avoids avatar uncanniness entirely.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 plans: Free (no commercial licence); Starter $6/month, 30,000 credits, commercial use and Instant Voice Cloning; Creator $22/month (first month $11 promo), about 121,000 credits, Professional Voice Cloning; Pro $99/month, about 600,000 credits, 44.1 kHz PCM via API. Annual billing is 10× monthly. Some older sources list $5 Starter or 100k/500k credits, which conflicts. — [cartesia](https://www.cartesia.ai/learn/elevenlabs-pricing); [bigvu](https://bigvu.tv/blog/elevenlabs-pricing-2026-plan-worth/); [dev.to](https://dev.to/stimlau/elevenlabs-pricing-explained-free-starter-creator-and-pro-in-2026-4p6b); [costbench ElevenLabs](https://costbench.com/software/ai-voice-tools/elevenlabs/)
- PVC verification needs a live captcha read with 4 attempts, then manual help-centre verification. PVCs can be shared in the Voice Library, but Instant clones cannot be shared publicly. Voices stay exclusive to the account by default. — [ElevenLabs docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/voices)
- ElevenLabs publishes a voice guide covering accents and voice types, including a Voice Library. Specific en-GB voice counts were not in the snippets. — [ElevenLabs voice guide](https://elevenlabs.io/voice-guide)

### Inferences
- A practical combination is: script → ElevenLabs narration in the author's PVC or a British library voice → timed over slides or screen recordings in a free editor (e.g. DaVinci Resolve, Clipchamp, Descript). Optionally, import the ElevenLabs audio into HeyGen or Synthesia to drive an avatar for intro and outro shots only.

## 6. How learners respond to AI avatar instructors (evidence 2023–2026)

### Takeaway
Across several controlled studies, **learning outcomes with AI-avatar instructors are about equal to human-instructor videos**. But **engagement, social presence and perceived human-likeness tend to be lower**, and some students find avatars "distracting, uncomfortable". At least one study links lower human-likeness to lower motivation and, indirectly, lower retention. Evidence on uncanny valley is mixed, and disclosure detection may reduce trust.

### Cited Findings
- **Leiker et al. 2023** (AIED 2023; arXiv 2304.03784): 83 adults were randomised to a filmed instructor or a realistic synthetic-presenter video. Both groups improved (p<.001), and gains did not differ (p=.80). Perceptions did not differ. The sample is small, so this is weak evidence of equivalence. Commercial co-author interest has been noted. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03784v1); [Warwick project page](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/cross_fac/eduport/edufund/projects/yang/projects/generative-ai-for-learning-investigating-the-potential-of-synthetic-learning-videos/)
- **Netland et al. 2025** (*Computers & Education*): 447 participants. Learning outcomes were equivalent between AI-generated (LLM script plus avatar) and human-made teaching videos. Participants reported a slightly more favourable subjective experience with human instructors. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360131524001787); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384280621_Comparing_Human-made_and_AI-generated_Teaching_Videos_An_Experimental_Study_on_Learning_Effects)
- **Arkün-Kocadere & Çağlar-Özhan 2024** (IRRODL 25(3)): 108 undergraduates. **Lower video engagement with the AI instructor, same academic performance**. Focus group students called the AI instructor "distracting, uncomfortable, and disconnecting". This was less so when the topic interested them. — [IRRODL](https://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/view/7815)
- **Xu et al. 2025** (*BJET* 56:1463–1487): AI-generated instructional videos versus recorded videos. Per secondary sources: comparable overall learning, higher retention for AI and similar transfer, but **recorded videos had stronger social presence**. Not verified against the full text. — [BJET](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjet.13530); [Tiffin summary](https://www.tiffin.edu/wp-content/uploads/Article-From-Recording-to-AI-Instructional-Video.pdf)
- **2026 2×2 study** (*Education and Information Technologies*, n=130), real or AI instructor × human or synthetic voice: no direct effects on motivation or retention. AI instructors were perceived as less human-like, which reduced motivation and so indirectly reduced retention. — [Springer 10.1007/s10639-026-14085-y](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-026-14085-y)
- **2026 eye-tracking study** (*EAIT*): comparable visual attention for AI and human instructors. Any instructor image, human or AI, increased cognitive load in procedural videos. It reported higher learning outcomes in the AI-instructor condition. — [Springer 10.1007/s10639-026-14149-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-026-14149-z)
- **2026 *Scientific Reports***: students preferred personalised AI-generated videos over non-personalised human-recorded videos. Title only seen. — [Nature Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-52798-9)
- Adult learner recall and recognition: no significant difference between conditions. — [arXiv 2412.10384](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.10384)
- 2025 rapid review of AI-generated instructional videos in higher education. Title only seen. — [Frontiers in Computer Science](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2025.1721093/epub)
- Uncanny valley and trust are mixed. Acta Psychologica 2025 (humanoid versus audio-only tutors) posed the uncanny valley question. A secondary summary reports Baake, Schmitt & Metag 2025 found **no** uncanny valley effect, with more realistic avatars seen as more trustworthy. A summary of Lind 2024 reports that learners who **detected** that the presenter was AI had lower trust, engagement and satisfaction despite similar retention. The last two come from a secondary Substack and are unverified. — [Acta Psychologica](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691825003105); [Dr Philippa Hardman Substack](https://drphilippahardman.substack.com/p/your-synthetic-educator-looks-perfect)

### Inferences
- For a free VE course, an avatar presenter is unlikely to harm learning, but it may reduce engagement and completion compared with the real author on camera.
- A **digital twin of the real author**, disclosed as such, may keep more social presence and trust than an anonymous stock avatar. This is untested in the literature found.
- Because any instructor image can add cognitive load in procedural content (2026 eye-tracking study), keep the avatar for intro and outro or a small picture-in-picture, and let diagrams and screens dominate during technical explanation.

### Gaps
- No study found measured **course completion or dropout** specifically for AI-avatar MOOCs; the IRRODL study measured engagement.
- No study compared digital twins of the actual instructor with stock avatars.
- Effect sizes were not verified against full texts.

## 7. Recommended production workflow and time/cost for a 2–5 minute technical explainer

### Takeaway
Rendering an avatar video takes minutes and a few dollars in credits. The real cost is the author's time on script, visuals and review. Vendor sources put agency-produced training video at $1,000–$10,000 per finished minute and the avatar credit cost at about $2.40/minute (HeyGen Avatar V). No independent source gave script-to-publish hours. The estimates below are clearly labelled as inference.

### Cited Findings
- Agency corporate or training video costs $1,000–$10,000 per finished minute and typically takes 4–6 weeks from brief to delivery. Avatar videos render "in less than 30 minutes from simple text scripts". This is vendor marketing. — [HeyGen blog: avatars vs traditional](https://www.heygen.com/blog/ai-avatars-vs-traditional-video-production-cost); [HeyGen blog: AI video production costs](https://www.heygen.com/blog/ai-video-production-costs)
- Avatar V costs 48 credits/minute at $0.05/credit, about $2.40/minute, so a 5-minute video is about $12 in credits plus the subscription. — [HeyGen blog](https://www.heygen.com/blog/ai-video-production-costs)
- The key advantage is that updates take "a few clicks" plus re-rendering, rather than a reshoot. — [Colossyan blog](https://www.colossyan.com/posts/ai-avatars-vs-traditional-videos); [guidde](https://www.guidde.com/knowledge-hub/ai-avatar-generation-training-videos-guide)
- Widely quoted "70% cost / 80% time reduction" figures come from vendor blogs and are unverified. — [HeyGen blog: reduce costs](https://www.heygen.com/blog/how-to-reduce-video-production-costs-with-ai)

### Inferences (workflow; estimates are mine, not sourced)
1. **Script** (British English, about 130–150 words/minute, so about 400–700 words for 3–5 minutes). Draft with an LLM from the module text, then edit by the author. Estimate 1–2 hours.
2. **Visuals**: export the module's existing diagrams or slides, and record screen demos of templates or calculators. Estimate 1–2 hours.
3. **Voice**: either the author's ElevenLabs PVC (Creator, $22/month) or the platform's built-in en-GB voice, or the twin's cloned voice in HeyGen.
4. **Presenter**: HeyGen Creator (about $24–29/month) with a self-recorded digital twin, made once, about 1 hour to record plus a consent clip. Use the avatar in about 20–30% of screen time (intro, transitions, summary) and full-screen visuals for the technical parts.
5. **Assemble and caption** in the platform or a free editor. Add a burned-in or on-screen "AI-generated presenter" label (EU AI Act Art. 50) and en-GB captions. Estimate 0.5–1 hour.
6. **Technical review**: watch the full video and check numbers and terms. Estimate 0.5 hour.
7. **Optional extras**: NotebookLM Brief or Audio Overview as a free supplementary recap after review; HeyGen translation for other languages.
- Rough total is about **4–6 author-hours and under $15 marginal cost per 3–5 minute video**, plus about $25–50/month in subscriptions while producing. Lower-cost alternative: ElevenLabs narration over slides only, about $6–22/month and no avatar.

### Gaps
- No independent time-and-motion data for AI avatar explainer production was found.
- Quality of auto-generated slide layouts from document-to-video features (Synthesia, Colossyan) for engineering diagrams was not evaluated by any source found.
