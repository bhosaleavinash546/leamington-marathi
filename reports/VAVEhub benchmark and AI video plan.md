# Move VAVEhub from reading to doing

VAVEhub does not need much more content. The two courses already hold more than the best free competitors offer. What they lack is proof that learners can do value engineering, practice that keeps working after the first read, and data to show you what is happening. In priority order: publish Step B with the stale "65-question" copy fixed; switch on the analytics that are already coded; stop sending guide readers into an unannounced sign-up wall; then spend the next quarter on one capstone product carried through the whole job plan, labs for the modules that have none, a spaced-review queue and a clearly worded credential. The best-evidenced learning practices are retrieval practice, spacing, worked examples that fade into problems, and scaffolded simulation. All of them suit a static site plus Supabase, and none needs an AI tutor. Daily streaks, public leaderboards and a chat tutor have weak evidence at your scale and should wait or be dropped. AI video is worth using, but only for narrow jobs. The research finds that avatar presenters teach about as well as filmed humans but engage people less. So use your own voice and your own diagrams for the teaching, a labelled HeyGen digital twin of you for short openings and closings, NotebookLM only for recaps you have checked, and Google Veo 3.1 for a handful of generic, labelled b-roll shots. OpenAI's Sora is gone. The twelve-video plan below costs roughly $250–350 in subscriptions and generation fees (prices unverified) and about 75 hours of your time over two to three months.

*How to read this report.* Findings about the site come from a code audit of the repository at commit `16a93a4` on 14 October 2026, which included a local run of the repository's own tests (208 of 208 passed). Those findings are cited by file name rather than by link. Every outside source was read through search-result summaries, because the research tools could not open vendor or regulator pages directly. Prices in particular come from third-party 2026 reviews, often disagree with each other, and are flagged as unverified. Check each one on the vendor's own page before you pay.

## The content is strong; the gaps are friction, proof and blind spots

The first review's fixes have left you with a course most commercial providers would recognise as serious. The VE Academy's 13 modules each follow the same pattern: objectives, worked example, case study, pitfalls, takeaways and an assignment. Each module also has two shuffled quick checks that gate completion and a scenario challenge of six to eight questions. On top of that sit glossary tooltips (43 rendered), course search, a 20-question practice exam drawn from a 120-question pool, 20 flashcards, printable summaries, British-voice narration with paragraph highlighting, and labs in six modules. Step B adds a server-marked 30-question exam (two per module plus four applied questions), an 80% pass mark, a one-hour wait between attempts, per-module feedback on failure and an answer review on a pass (`training.js`, `vh-exam-ui.js`, `supabase-exam.sql`). That pass mark is stricter than the 70% IBM uses on its Coursera certificates ([IBM Skills Network](https://portals.skills.network/docs/learning-content/course)), which helps credibility. **Step B is still only on your machine.** The live site will not change until you run `supabase-exam.sql` and load the private question bank. If the bank is empty, the exam page shows "The exam couldn't be loaded just now".

The problems sit around the content rather than in it. **Seven of the ten guide pages send readers to Academy modules that are locked behind sign-up**, under a card that promises a "free, narrated and interactive" course and says nothing about an account. No guide links to the Leaders course at all (`tools/build.py`). The sign-up gate and the sign-up page still advertise a "65-question exam bank", and a test (`tests/suites/academy.js`, line 153) checks for that old figure, so fixing the copy will break the test until it is updated. The course length appears three ways: about 6 hours in the catalogue, about 6.5 hours on certificates, and 5 hours 43 minutes if you add up the module times. A certificate should not contradict its own course page. The exam unlocks only after all 13 modules are marked complete, but that rule is checked in the browser, not in `start_exam()` on the server. "My learning" always points to the Academy, even for someone taking only Leaders (`vh-account.js`). Reading the code also suggests that a returning signed-in learner lands on Module 1 rather than where they stopped, because the account loads after the start page is chosen. That was not tested with a real account.

The media mix is uneven. **Five Academy modules have no video at all (m3, m6, m7, m8, m12), and m11, the AI module, has only an embedded GM clip.** Seven modules have no lab: m1, m2, m3, m8, m11, m12 and m13. The longest stretches of unbroken prose run to 914 words in m5, 830 in m4, 697 in m11 and 657 in m6, which is three to five minutes of reading with nothing to do. In Leaders, l0, l5 and l6 have no video. The readiness check and pilot picker in l5 and l6 forget their results as soon as the page closes (`leaders-labs.js`).

Gamification is mostly decoration. Points are 100 per completed module plus 800 for the exam, so they tell a learner nothing the progress bar does not. Labs, challenges and the practice exam earn nothing. The two courses are also out of step with each other. Leaders is open without sign-in and resumes automatically, but has no search, glossary, practice exam, flashcards or module summaries. The Academy has all of those but locks everything after Module 1.

The biggest weakness is that **you cannot see most of what learners do**. GoatCounter, Buttondown, Giscus, Formspree and Calendly are all coded but switched off, because their keys in `site-config.js` are empty. The admin dashboard shows only signed-in learners. Today you cannot measure anonymous visits, guide-to-sign-up conversion, video plays, narration use, lab use, wrong-answer patterns, search terms or time per module. Leaders learners who never sign in are invisible. The exam table records which questions were served but **not which options people chose**, so you cannot find a badly worded question in the bank. Accessibility basics pass the repository's axe checks at WCAG 2 AA, and pages fit a 375 px phone. However, the eight self-hosted videos carry burned-in subtitles and no caption track (`<track>`), so captions cannot be resized or switched off. The Academy is also one 1.27 MB page with 5,472 elements, which is slow on mobile data.

Of the open roadmap from the first review, only the LinkedIn add-to-profile button is done, and it passes a company name but no company ID, so LinkedIn shows no logo. Giscus, the toolkit spreadsheet links and the Leaders readiness check are partly done. Everything else in C to G is still open: analytics, a CPD log, the gated links, worksheets for m7, m9, m12 and m13, a capstone, a drag-and-drop FAST builder, labs for m3, m8 and m13, the Leaders executive pack, the new content topics, spaced recall, goals, reminders, an AI tutor and testimonials.

## Five lessons from the best platforms, ranked by strength of evidence

The benchmark covered MOOCs (Coursera, edX, Udemy, LinkedIn Learning, MasterClass, Maven), practice-first apps (Brilliant, Khan Academy, Duolingo, DataCamp, Codecademy), professional training (SAVE International, IVM, ASQ, IASSC, GoLeanSixSigma, PMI) and corporate L&D systems (Docebo, Degreed, 360Learning). These platforms make many claims. What separates the reliable from the unreliable is who measured the result. Peer-reviewed findings and randomised trials sit at one end. Platform-reported uplift figures with no stated method sit at the other. The table sorts the main practices on that basis.

| Practice | Best evidence | Quality | VAVEhub today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Practice testing and spaced review | Dunlosky et al. 2013 rate both "high utility"; Roediger & Karpicke 2006 found testing beat restudy at 2 days and 1 week; well-chosen gaps improved recall by up to 150% (Cepeda et al.) | Strong, independent | Quick checks and practice exam, but nothing brings old material back later |
| Interleaving mixed problems | 72% vs 38% on a delayed test (Rohrer 2014, d = 1.05) | Strong, mostly short-term | The practice exam already mixes modules |
| Worked examples that fade into problems | Renkl & Atkinson; expertise-reversal effect; adaptive fading beats fixed fading | Strong for novices | One worked example per module, never faded |
| Scaffolded simulation | Meta-analysis of 145 studies, g = 0.85 (Chernikova et al. 2020) | Strong | Labs in 6 of 13 Academy modules |
| Videos under about 6 minutes | 6.9 million edX sessions (Guo et al. 2014) | Strong but from 2014 | Existing clips are short; 5 modules have none |
| Fixed start dates, reminders | 45% vs 13% retention, fixed vs self-paced; simple deadline emails worked in an RCT | Moderate | Fully self-paced, no reminders |
| Streaks, XP, leagues | Duolingo A/B tests: +1.7% to +14% on retention measures; "3.6×" is correlational | Company data only | Points, ranks, badges, a streak label |
| AI tutor | Two-year Khanmigo RCT: no gain beyond ordinary Khan practice | Strong, and negative | None |

### Practice beats presentation, and VAVEhub is half-way there

The research case for active practice is the strongest in the benchmark. Across 225 STEM studies, active learning raised exam scores by about 0.47 standard deviations, and students in traditional lectures were 1.5 times more likely to fail ([Freeman et al. 2014](https://cdn.serc.carleton.edu/files/nagt/profdev/twp/sanbernardino/freeman_et_al._2014.pdf)). Practice testing and distributed practice are the only two study techniques rated "high utility" in the standard review. Rereading and highlighting are rated low ([Dunlosky et al., via APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html)). Testing beats restudy once the test is days rather than minutes away ([Roediger & Karpicke 2006](https://psychology.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/216/2019/03/Roediger-Karpicke-2006.pdf)). The best gap between sessions grows with how long you want people to remember ([Cepeda et al. 2009](https://home.cs.colorado.edu/~mozer/Research/Selected%20Publications/reprints/Cepedaetal2009.pdf)). Simulation-based learning with scaffolding produced an overall effect of **g = 0.85**. Novices gained most from worked examples, experienced learners from reflection ([Chernikova et al. 2020](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/84298/1/0034654320933544.pdf)). Worked examples help beginners most when the steps are removed one at a time ([MIT Open Learning](https://openlearning.mit.edu/mit-faculty/research-based-learning-findings/worked-and-faded-examples)). The same examples become redundant, even harmful, for people who already know the method ([Kalyuga, expertise reversal](https://faculty.engineering.asu.edu/mre/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2020/02/Exp_Rev_LI06.pdf)).

That evidence is why Brilliant, Khan Academy and DataCamp have moved from watching to manipulating. It also points to what VAVEhub is missing. You have retrieval at the moment of learning, but nothing that brings function definitions or FAST logic back a week later. You have worked examples, but none that hand over to the learner step by step. The practice exam already interleaves questions across modules, which the evidence supports ([Rohrer 2014](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2014-rohrer.pdf)). One warning from Brilliant applies directly to FAST diagrams. A long-term reviewer complained that some answers had no single right answer, so passing meant guessing what the app wanted ([cedarlila review](https://cedarlila.substack.com/p/a-review-brilliant)). More than one valid FAST diagram exists for most products. Any automatic checking must accept tolerances and explain why alternatives are acceptable.

### Short segments matter more than production polish

The most-cited video study found engagement falls sharply after about six minutes. Informal talking-head and hand-drawn styles held attention better than polished studio lectures ([Guo, Kim & Rubin 2014](https://up.csail.mit.edu/other-pubs/las2014-pguo-engagement.pdf)). Coursera's reported guidance is lessons of about 30 minutes that end in an assessment, inside modules of one to two hours ([Technion, quoting Coursera](https://mooctech.net.technion.ac.il/resources/mooc-structure/)). Mayer's multimedia principles favour cutting decoration (coherence) and highlighting what matters (signalling), and both have repeated experimental support ([Dartmouth summary](https://services.dartmouth.edu/TDClient/1806/Portal/KB/PrintArticle?ID=171655)). For VAVEhub this means the 900-word runs in m4 and m5 need a check, a diagram or a lab step part-way through, more than they need a video. It also means the new videos should be short and cheap-looking rather than long and slick.

### Completion follows commitment and structure, not badges

Self-paced online courses lose most of their learners. The median completion rate across 221 MOOCs was 12.6% ([Jordan 2015](https://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/view/2112/3364)). In MIT and Harvard's edX data, more than half of registrants never opened the course, yet 46% of paying "verified" learners finished ([Reich & Ruipérez-Valiente](https://tsl.mit.edu/research/the-mooc-pivot/)). The difference is commitment, not content. A programming MOOC kept 45% of learners on a fixed schedule against 13% self-paced ([IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9274023/)). In a randomised trial, the simplest reminder (a plain email naming the next deadline) worked best ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10611998)). Asking learners to choose a study time did nothing, or slightly harmed outcomes ([Baker, Evans & Dee 2016](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2332858416674007)). Cohort platforms claim 75% to 96% completion, but those figures come from investors and instructors with no published method ([a16z](https://a16z.com/announcement/investing-in-maven/)).

Gamification evidence is almost all Duolingo's own. A streak-milestone animation raised day-7 activity among new learners by 1.7%. Separating the daily goal from the streak raised day-14 retention by 3.3% ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)). The famous "3.6 times more likely to finish" figure for a seven-day streak is a correlation, not a cause ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)). Optimising notifications across millions of users added 0.5% to daily activity ([Yancey & Settles](https://research.Duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf)). Leagues have the clearest record of harm, with users reporting stress and point-chasing in place of learning ([arXiv 2203.16175](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16175)). A niche professional course could not fill a 30-person league anyway. For working engineers the better design is a weekly goal, a dated "start together" intake each quarter, one plain reminder email, and private milestones tied to real skills such as "first FAST diagram built". Public rankings should be left out.

### Credibility comes from applied evidence and honest positioning

Credentials people trust stack three proofs: approved training, evidence of real practice, and an exam, usually followed by renewal. To reach IVM's Qualified Value Associate (QVA) level, a learner needs an approved VM1 course, **two value studies as a team member** and a multiple-choice assessment, all under BS EN 12973:2020 ([IVM CB-103](https://ivm.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/CB-103-V4E-VM-Training-and-Certification-System-FINAL-APPROVED-v15.2-20240314.pdf)). SAVE's route to Value Methodology Associate (VMA) goes through its licensed VMF1 programme. VMF1A, the online part, costs **$300 for members, $350 for non-members and $100 for students** ([SAVE](https://www.value-eng.org/page/vmf1program)). The 2018 manual set Certified Value Specialist (CVS) entry at six value studies and 240 contact hours ([SAVE manual, 2018](https://cdn.ymaws.com/www.value-eng.org/resource/collection/41067BF5-2271-4859-A4AE-E77BB72D0036/SAVE_Certification_Program_Manual_-_15th_Edition_-_effective_5-1-18_FINAL.pdf)). A newer manual dated October 2025 exists but could not be read. CVS renewal now runs on a four-year cycle ([SAVE](https://www.value-eng.org/page/CVSrenewal)).

No independent online course can stand in for those routes, and VAVEhub must not imply that it does. It can do what GoLeanSixSigma does: run one practical example project through every phase, give away the templates, and add a checklist at each phase gate ([GLSS project approach](https://goleansixsigma.com/project-requirement-green-belt-training-certification/); [GLSS tollgate checklist](https://goleansixsigma.com/yellow-belt-tollgate-checklist/)). It can state learning hours on the certificate, as LinkedIn Learning does for accountants' continuing-education (CPE) credit ([LinkedIn Learning](https://learning.linkedin.com/blog/whats-new/cpas-_-earn-your-continuing-professional-education--cpe--units-a)). It can add a note saying that holders of the PMI project-management credential (PMP) may self-report the hours as professional development units (PDUs). PMI cannot pre-approve PDUs from unregistered courses, but self-reporting is allowed ([projectmanagement.com](https://www.projectmanagement.com/discussion-topic/202255/pmi-webinars-differ-in-pdu-reporting--self-reported-vs--auto-reported-pdus)). The Miles Value Institute already sells a $99 function-analysis course listed with 3 PDUs ([Miles Value Institute](https://valuefoundation.teachable.com/courses)), so the market accepts the idea.

PMI's paid exam prep sells full-length mock exams plus short 10-question mini-exams ([PMI Study Hall Plus](https://www.pmi.org/shop/p-/digital-product/pmp-pmi-study-hall-plus-subscription/dp081)), and VAVEhub could offer the same free. Google's Skillshop certificates last a year and must be renewed ([Google](https://support.google.com/skillshop/answer/7378254?hl=en)), which gives people a reason to come back and builds in long-gap revision. Open Badges 3.0 turns a badge into a signed credential that can be checked without your server ([1EdTech](https://www.1edtech.org/article/1edtech-announces-major-improvements-educational-credentials)). On course pages, Maven's rubric is a sound guide: outcomes written as "you'll be able to… so you can…", a line per module saying what the learner will produce, an instructor credibility block, and **at least two named testimonials** ([Maven](https://help.maven.com/en/articles/6815473-landing-page-rubric)). VAVEhub's homepage has none of these.

### AI features: the platforms' numbers are their own

Coursera reports that learners using its Coach tutor had a 9.5% higher first-attempt quiz pass rate, but gives no control group or method ([Coursera](https://blog.coursera.org/coursera-coach-providing-essential-learner-support)). HeyGen reports that AI dubbing lifted Coursera course completion by 25%, which is a vendor case study ([HeyGen](https://www.heygen.com/customer-stories/coursera)). The strongest independent test runs the other way. A two-year randomised trial in 18 Tennessee schools found Khanmigo added nothing beyond ordinary Khan Academy practice, largely because students rarely used it for real help ([NBER w35620](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35620); [Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-khanmigo-math-ai-tutor/)). Coursera's own data show that AI marking was stricter than peer marking: 72% passed first time against 88% ([Coursera](https://blog.coursera.org/ai-grading-in-peer-reviews-enhancing-courseras-learning-experience-with-faster-high-quality-feedback)). For VAVEhub, the useful lesson is the narrow "explain my mistake" feature that Codecademy uses. You can get most of that benefit free, without running any AI, by writing specific feedback for the common VE errors: a function written as a noun rather than verb plus noun, "how" and "why" reversed, overhead applied to material twice.

On the corporate side, employers' learning teams expect courses they can load into their own learning management system (SCORM packages), team sign-up and a view for managers ([xapi.com](https://xapi.com/cmi5/comparison-of-scorm-xapi-and-cmi5/); [Docebo community](https://community.docebo.com/product-q-a-7/assigning-course-as-manager-11832)). A light version of that comes later. It is how a free course gets into companies.

## AI video earns a place, but not as a stand-in for you

### What the evidence says about avatar teachers

The research on synthetic presenters agrees on one point and splits on another. On learning, AI presenters match humans. In a 447-person experiment, people learned equally well from AI-generated and human-made teaching videos, though they slightly preferred the human ([Netland et al. 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360131524001787)). A smaller randomised study found the same ([Leiker et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03784v1)). On engagement, AI presenters do worse. Undergraduates watching an AI instructor engaged less, scored the same, and called the presenter "distracting, uncomfortable, and disconnecting" ([IRRODL 2024](https://www.irrodl.org/index.php/irrodl/article/view/7815)). A 2026 study found AI instructors seemed less human, which lowered motivation and, through that, retention ([Springer 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-026-14085-y)). An eye-tracking study found that any instructor image, human or AI, added mental load in step-by-step videos ([Springer 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-026-14149-z)). One secondary summary reports that learners who spotted an undisclosed AI presenter trusted it less. That claim is unverified ([Hardman](https://drphilippahardman.substack.com/p/your-synthetic-educator-looks-perfect)).

Three practical rules follow. First, AI video will not damage learning, so it is safe to use. Second, it will not lift engagement on its own, so it is not a substitute for labs or practice. Third, the presenter should appear briefly. Let your diagrams, templates and lab screens carry the technical explanation, and keep the face for the opening, the hand-offs and the close. No study has compared a disclosed digital twin of the real author with a stock avatar. It is reasonable to expect a twin of you to keep more trust than an anonymous face, but that is a judgement, not a finding.

There is also a cheaper and more authentic option that the vendors do not push. Your phone, a window and a decent microphone. Guo's study found informal talking-head video held attention better than studio production ([Guo et al.](https://up.csail.mit.edu/other-pubs/las2014-pguo-engagement.pdf)). Use your real face for the two trailers, which are the videos that most need trust. You need to record yourself anyway to build a twin, so the trailers cost little extra.

### What AI b-roll can and cannot show

Generated video in 2026 is good at mood and bad at facts. Reviewers find on-screen text comes out garbled "more often than not". Hands and fine physics still give the model away ([pieroperilli.com](https://pieroperilli.com/en/blog/ai-video-generation-2026-what-actually-works)), and fingers multiply or flicker between frames ([lovart.ai](https://www.lovart.ai/blog/ai-video-generator-2026-review)). The models learn visual patterns, not mechanics. They make cause-and-effect errors and tend to show physical actions succeeding ([benchr](https://benchr.org/articles/best-ai-for-video)). Keeping one character consistent across shots is still hard. Wide factory establishing shots, slow moves past machinery, engineers at a table seen from behind, and blurred sticky notes on a wall are likely to work. Close-ups of hands doing a teardown, readable notes, cost charts, gauges, correct press or CNC motion, logos and a recurring character are likely to fail. In a training course a wrong mechanism on screen is an accuracy error, not just an ugly shot. Generate a still first and animate it, ask for blank screens and paper, keep shots to 4–8 seconds, and add every number, label and diagram yourself in the editor.

### Labelling and ethics rules

The EU AI Act's transparency duties (Article 50) have applied since **2 August 2026**, and the July 2026 Digital Omnibus did not delay them ([legalithm](https://www.legalithm.com/en/blog/eu-ai-act-what-applies-2-august-2026)). Anyone who publishes a "deepfake" must label it. The Act defines a deepfake as AI-made content resembling real people, places or events that "would falsely appear authentic". The guidance says the publisher's intent does not matter ([SRD Rechtsanwälte](https://www.srd-rechtsanwaelte.de/en/blog/deepfake-labelling-under-the-ai-act-what-the-new-eu-guidance-clarifies)). The lighter rule for artistic or fictional work does not cover content whose main purpose is to inform ([SRD](https://www.srd-rechtsanwaelte.de/en/blog/labelling-requirements-ai-act-deepfakes)). Fines reach €15m or 3% of turnover ([Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/eu-ai-act-commission-confirms-transparency-code-of-practice-as-adequate-and-publishes-final-version-of-its-guidelines-on-transparency-obligations)). The UK has no equivalent duty, but the site is open to EU learners. A realistic twin of you, or a photoreal factory shot, plausibly falls within the definition, and labelling costs nothing. Label everything.

Use the same wording every time. Show a lower-third caption in the first five seconds of any twin segment, for example: *"AI-generated presenter: a digital likeness of [your name], made with his consent. Script written and checked by him."* Show a small corner caption on generated footage: *"AI-generated illustrative footage."* Put a one-line "How this video was made" note under each video, linking to a short "How VAVEhub uses AI" page. That page should also cover the existing module narration, which is already a synthetic British voice (en-GB-SoniaNeural). When uploading to YouTube, tick the "altered or synthetic content" box for any photoreal scene or twin ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). Never strip the SynthID or C2PA marks the tools add. LinkedIn shows a Content Credentials badge only when those marks survive in the file ([Tuesday Logic](https://tuesdaylogic.com.au/insights/ai-content-is-now-visible-on-linkedin-what-this-means-for-businesses/)). Keep a simple log of each shot: tool, model, prompt, date and who checked it.

Some things should never appear. Do not generate scenes of a named company's plant, products, logo or staff: no "Tesla gigafactory line", no "GM engineers". Those meet the deepfake test directly and add trade-mark and false-depiction risk. Mention real companies only in narration and text, backed by the sourced case material you already use. Do not use a stock avatar presented as a VE expert. Do not let the twin say anything you did not write. Do not put anyone else's face or voice in a video without their recorded consent. Keep your own copy of the source footage. Synthesia documents how to delete your avatar data ([Synthesia](https://help.synthesia.io/en/articles/15453102-avatar-policy-foundation)). HeyGen's process is less clear, and users report deleted avatars reappearing ([HeyGen forum](https://docs.heygen.com/discuss/675f1d6d649326001170734e)). If you leave HeyGen, ask support in writing, citing your UK GDPR rights. On copyright, the US Copyright Office says prompts alone do not make you an author ([Copyright Alliance](https://copyrightalliance.org/ai-report-part-2-copyrightability/)). The UK government proposed in March 2026 to end protection for wholly computer-generated works ([HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/ip/2026-03/uk-government-report-on-copyright-and-ai-concludes-more-evidence-is-needed-although-s9-3-cdpa-could-go)). What you own, and what protects the course, is your script, your diagrams and your edit.

### Accessibility rules

WCAG 2.2 at level AA requires captions for recorded video (1.2.2) and audio description of important visual content (1.2.5) ([W3C 1.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-prerecorded.html); [W3C 1.2.5](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/audio-description-prerecorded.html)). You can meet the second without a separate description track: write the narration so it says what the screen shows ("material is 62% of the cost, so that is where we look first"). Then nothing important exists only as a picture. Ship every video, old and new, with a WebVTT captions file (`<track kind="captions" srclang="en-GB">`) rather than relying on burned-in subtitles ([W3C WebVTT](https://www.w3.org/TR/webvtt1/)). Keep burned-in text only for labels that belong to the graphic. Correct auto-captions by hand for FAST, VAVE, TRIZ, DFMA and the other terms that speech recognition mangles. Keep the HTML transcript under every video. Keep each video under six minutes. Never autoplay with sound, and make any silent looping b-roll pausable. If you later dub into another language, each dubbed track needs its own matching captions.

## Conclusion

The benchmark changes the question. VAVEhub already has more content than learners finish, so writing more is not the job. The job is to give learners something to do with the content, bring it back to them a week later, and turn finishing into evidence an employer or a SAVE or IVM assessor can respect. The capstone product, the faded labs, the spaced-review queue and honest credential wording all serve that one aim, and all of them fit your static site and Supabase. The most useful hours in the next fortnight are dull ones: publishing Step B, removing the stale "65", switching on GoatCounter and recording which options people choose. Until those are done, you are improving a course whose learners you cannot see.

AI video fits into that plan as a production tool, not a teacher. The research says a synthetic presenter will not harm learning but will not create engagement either. So the case for a twin is practical: you can re-render a module video after a content fix without re-filming, and the presenter appears briefly. The rest of the time your diagrams and labs carry the teaching, and every synthetic element is labelled. Run the m6 pilot, read your own feedback data, and let that decide whether the twin stays. The analytics you switch on this month will tell you which parts of this report were right for your learners and which were not.
