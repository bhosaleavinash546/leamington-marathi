# BrainSpark — 360° audit, August 2026

A full review of the platform as built: every feature exercised, the code read
adversarially, the accuracy claims re-measured, and the result compared against
the tools BrainSpark would be sold against.

Written to be useful rather than flattering. Where the tool is good it says so
with evidence; where it overstates itself it says that with evidence too.

---

## Verdict

**The engineering discipline is real, and it is the product's genuine
differentiator. The claims made *about* that discipline had outrun it.**

Three things are true at once, and holding all three is the honest picture:

1. **The deterministic core is better than the market's default.** The
   should-cost engine returns `engine: "deterministic"`, a live commodity price
   with the date it was fetched, an FX source labelled as a static reference,
   a carbon figure that volunteers what it understates, and four traceable
   assumption lines. The DFM engine returns `score: null` — not 100 — when
   nothing could be evaluated, and across 35 process families on a real part it
   returned 48 fails, 78 passes and **122 rules honestly marked not-evaluated,
   every one with a reason**. That is a tool built by someone who has been
   burned by a confident wrong number.

2. **The honesty stopped at the door.** The artefacts that *leave* the building
   — the Excel workbook to procurement, the PowerPoint to management, the RFQ
   pack to suppliers — carried none of it. An idea the engine had explicitly
   **contradicted** reached a supplier reading exactly as authoritative as one
   it had confirmed. Meanwhile the ranking that decides which ideas surface
   first was driven by the model's own unverified savings claim, so an idea that
   overstated itself outranked one the engine had checked. Both are now fixed.

3. **The accuracy is materially weaker than the CI dashboard suggests.** The
   gated benchmark reports 100% hit-rate and 8.3% MAPE. The genuinely held-out
   set — parts authored *after* calibration was frozen, and not run in CI —
   reports **78.6% and 20.9%**, with a −7.3% bias, meaning the engine reads
   *low* and therefore **overstates achievable savings**. That set was in the
   repo, documented, and unmonitored.

**Have you built something good?** Yes — the hard part is done and it is
distinctive. The deterministic engines, the three-state DFM discipline and the
provenance architecture are things aPriori and Teamcenter users complain about
not having. **Is it world-class yet?** No. It is roughly 70% of the way, and the
missing 30% is not more features — it is closing the gap between what the tool
knows and what it says, and getting the absolute accuracy up from "directionally
useful" to "quotable".

---

## Method

- **Static**: three parallel adversarial passes over feature wiring, the
  hallucination surface, and exports/measurement debt across ~59k lines.
- **Live**: a throwaway server on a temp DB, driven with a real API key through
  the should-cost, DFM, DFA, TRIZ and innovation engines on real STEP fixtures.
- **Measured**: every benchmark re-run, including the four that CI does not
  gate, plus the ideation eval — which had **never once completed** (see F-12).
- **Compared**: aPriori, Siemens Teamcenter PCM, Boothroyd Dewhurst DFMA,
  DFMPro, Costimator, ITONICS, and the TRIZ+AI research frontier.

Severity: **S1** = a wrong or misleading number reaches a third party ·
**S2** = misleads the user inside the tool · **S3** = quality/correctness defect
with limited blast radius.

### F-32 — the auditor made the same mistake the audit was looking for

**Severity: S1 against this document, not against the product.**

**What happened.** F-9 was produced by running `scripts/threshold-audit.mjs`,
reading its summary — *"212 unaudited · 6% primary document read · 27 rules
CLAIM A NAMED STANDARD that nobody has read first-hand"* — and reporting it as
the sourcing position of the rule catalogue. The script measures something else:
how many rules appear in `docs/threshold-audit.json`, the curation register. Its
own header states the distinction plainly: *"a grade is a statement ABOUT a
citation, not a record that anybody checked it."*

**Why it survived.** Three things lined up:

  * The number was *plausible* and *unflattering*. An audit looking for
    overstatement is primed to accept a finding that says the tool overstates,
    and applies less scrutiny than to one that says it does not.
  * **The product's own label agreed with it.** `SOURCE_GRADE` told readers
    "NAMED STANDARD, not read first-hand" for every standard-named rule. The
    corroboration was real, and it was wrong for 36 of 38 rules.
  * The contradicting evidence was in the file listing from the first
    orientation command — `nadca-die-casting.mjs`, `iso-8062-4.mjs`,
    `din-16742.mjs`. Six modules, 1,622 lines. Never questioned.

**Cost.** A false finding in the register, a false claim in the director
scorecard, and a request that the user obtain four standards documents they had
already supplied and which had already been read and encoded.

**What was done.**

  * F-9 retracted above, with the measured position in its place.
  * `threshold-audit.mjs` now reports the two axes under separate headings and
    states in terms that not-reviewed is an audit-trail gap rather than a claim
    the threshold is unsourced.
  * The customer-facing `SOURCE_GRADE` label is corrected — it was understating
    the tool's own rigour.
  * The single conflated CI ratchet is replaced by three named gates.
  * **Every one of the other 30 findings was re-swept** for the same signature —
    reported from a tool's output rather than measured. Result: 30 hold, 1 was
    wrong (this one). The full classification is in `docs/AUDIT-SWEEP.md`.

**The generalisable lesson**, which applies to the product as much as to the
audit: *a metric's name is not its definition.* `auditedPct` sounds like it
measures sourcing quality and measures review coverage. The fix is not more
care; it is making the two impossible to confuse at the point of reading.

## Competitive position

### Where BrainSpark is genuinely ahead

- **Provenance architecture.** Microsoft Research's 2026 VeriTrail work argues
  hallucination control in agentic workflows requires **per-step provenance,
  because errors propagate through chains** — and that provenance needs *a
  record of when a figure was last verified*. BrainSpark's stamp chain
  (`engineCheck`, `priorArt`, `tasteMatch`, `evidenceUnverified`, `rank.basis`)
  is exactly that pattern, built before the literature named it. Post-F-1 it now
  survives into the artefacts. **This is the wedge.**
- **Three-state DFM.** Pass / fail / **not-evaluated-with-a-reason**, and a null
  score rather than a flattering one. Rule-based incumbents (DFMPro, NX) report
  what they checked; they are not built to advertise what they could not.
- **Span.** aPriori, Teamcenter and DFMA cost things. ITONICS, HYPE and
  Brightidea generate and manage ideas. **Almost nobody does both**, and the
  loop — generate an idea, price it deterministically, feed the real quote back
  as calibration — is a defensible product shape.
- **Idea diversity is measured.** The research is explicit that AI ideation
  risks *less original, less diverse ideas and more fixation*. BrainSpark scores
  batch diversity (85–87 measured) and runs a quality-diversity archive against
  it. Competitors assert novelty; this measures it.
- **No CAD required.** Like DFMA and unlike aPriori, estimates work from
  parameters — an advantage aPriori users cite as a real friction.

### Where BrainSpark lags

| Gap | Against whom | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **Absolute accuracy.** 20.9% MAPE held-out; 34–39% on feature-based machining/stamping. aPriori/DFMA are trusted for quoting. | aPriori, DFMA, Costimator | **Critical** |
| **Process breadth.** 12 processes in the catalogue. DFMPro runs "thousands of rule-based checks"; DFMA ships global costing data across far more. | DFMPro, DFMA | High |
| **CAD integration.** Incumbents live *inside* Creo/SolidWorks/NX. BrainSpark is upload-based. | DFMPro, NX | High |
| **Validated cost data.** The reference prices are self-labelled "ILLUSTRATIVE anchors … NOT proprietary supplier quotes". Competitors sell curated regional rate libraries as the product. | all | **Critical** |
| **Enterprise plumbing.** No SSO, no PLM/ERP connectors, org model half-built. | Teamcenter, aPriori | High |
| **Scale.** aPriori users report slowness on large datasets; BrainSpark is single-instance SQLite, single writer. | all | Medium |

### The opening nobody has taken

The DFM market's own diagnosis: *"when an engineer asks whether a particular
feature is manufacturable, the answer often depends on information outside the
CAD model — past ECOs, supplier quality reports, cost data from previous
production runs, notes from a design review."* The stated 2026 trend is
platforms that **connect CAD rule-checking to organisational memory**.

BrainSpark already has the organisational memory layer — marketplace precedents,
the taste profile, quote-based calibration, the QD archive. **No incumbent has
that, and no ideation platform has the cost engines.** That intersection is the
product to build toward, and it is a stronger story than competing with aPriori
on process count.

## Improvement plan — what is left

Items 1, 2, 4 and 5 of the original "now" list, and most of "next", are done.
What remains is ranked by what it buys.

### The one that decides whether this is advisory or operational
1. **Close the three cost-model misses** (F-7b). Titanium, CFRP/RTM and
   high-volume CNC. The machinability attempt is written up in DECISIONS 50 with
   its measurement table; the blocker is data, not analysis. **Twenty quoted
   piece prices across aluminium, steel, brass and titanium** would let
   `cycleBase`/`cyclePerKg` be re-derived *with* a machinability term rather
   than having one bolted onto constants fitted without it. `calibration.mjs`
   already exists to absorb exactly that. Until then held-out MAPE stays near
   21% and the engine reads ~7% low, which means it overstates savings.

### Trust
2. **Work down the register backlog** (F-9 retracted; see F-32). `scripts/citation-worklist.mjs` orders
   the work; the CI ratchet stops it growing. Start with the 19 highest-exposure
   rules that cite **no document at all** — those need a citation sourced or an
   honest demotion, and no amount of buying standards will touch them.
3. **Finish the report-layer tests** (F-24). The provenance invariant and the
   PDF layout QA are in CI; the remaining ~5,700 lines need their pure decisions
   extracted into `.mjs` helpers, as `innovation-report-core.mjs` already is.

### Idea quality — the open product questions
4. **Measure the eval's variance** (F-30) before claiming any generation
   improvement again. Identical config on identical parts returned 46% and 20%.
5. **Decide what novelty means** (F-31). 83.1% of ideas are near-restatements of
   the corpus while batch diversity reads 87.7. Those measure different things
   and only one of them is currently gated.
6. **Decide the remaining orphans** (F-23) — surface or delete.

### The differentiated build, unchanged
7. **Close the calibration loop visibly** — quote-fed calibration exists but
   reports `applied: false`. Making "it gets more accurate the more you use it"
   a displayed, measured fact is the direct answer to aPriori's "average rates,
   not specific to your industry" weakness.
8. **Verification recency** — stamp when each figure was last verified and decay
   confidence with age. Commodity prices already carry `pricedAt`.
9. **Organisational memory as a first-class input** — ECOs, supplier quality
   history and past quotes feeding both DFM judgement and idea retrieval. The
   gap the market has named and nobody has filled.

## What is still unmeasured

Stated plainly, because the repo's own rule is "no asserted improvements":

- **Absolute idea quality.** Diversity and engine-check rate are measured;
  whether the ideas are *good* is not. The pairwise judge harness exists and has
  never been run against a frozen baseline.
- **The Horizon LLM layer** has no eval harness at all.
- **Report layout** is checked by scripts nobody runs in CI.
- **Every LLM-layer metric before this audit**, because the harness could not
  survive a network blip (F-12) — now fixed, so this debt is finally payable.
